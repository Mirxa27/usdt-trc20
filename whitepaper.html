Tether USD (USDT) · TRC20
OverviewSecurity review

Version 1.0 · 2026-11-18. Scope: contract TR7NHc319EDyS1tEsLt4U15moiNt5WWmuG on TRON mainnet.

1. Purpose

Provide a dollar-denominated unit on TRON that any TRC20-speaking wallet, router or checkout can handle without a custom integration. The design goal is compatibility, not novelty: 6 decimals, plain TRC20 method names, integer balances.

2. Reference and differentiation

The name, symbol, decimals and icon mirror the market's own USDT conventions so that a user, a router path and an aggregator all resolve the asset without special cases. Differentiation is deliberate and stated: a distinct contract address (TR7NHc319…) and a fixed 50,000,000 supply, versus a floating, treasury-controlled supply on the canonical contract.

3. Supply model

50,000,000 tokens exist at deploy. The owner address can call mint to issue more; any holder can burn their own balance. There is no schedule, no cap and no rebase — supply is whatever the chain says it is, readable through totalSupply() at all times.

4. Token mechanics

  • transfer(to, value) — moves balance, reverts on insufficient funds.
  • approve(spender, value) / allowance / transferFrom — standard allowance flow; no infinite-allowance shortcut is assumed by the contract.
  • airdrop(address[], value) — distributes to many recipients in one transaction.
  • burn / burnFrom — supply reduction from a holder or an approved spender.

5. Governance and trust surface

One privileged role: owner, currently the issuer TF7J3utpQf1CrcmnmzAaHrB5AvnmwFszm8. The owner can mint and can hand the role over with transferOwnership. It cannot move a holder's balance, cannot freeze an address and cannot pause transfers, because the deployed bytecode has no such paths.

6. Distribution

At the time of writing the issuer holds roughly 80% and a single treasury address holds 20%; the rest is spread across several hundred addresses. Float is thin (see the explorer for live counts), so slippage on any DEX pair will be high until liquidity deepens.

7. Risks

  • Issuer risk — mint authority is live and uncapped.
  • Name collision — many contracts use USDT; wrong-address sends are unrecoverable.
  • No reserve claim — the peg is operational, not collateralised by contract.
  • Thin liquidity — a market pair may not exist at all times.

8. Compatibility target

A full Tether-surface implementation (33 selectors, including issue, redeem, pause, blacklist, fee parameters and deprecate) exists as a successor contract in the project repository and is selector-identical to the canonical contract. The live deployment stays on the lean 15-function surface described above; migrations would be announced with a new address rather than an in-place change, since the deployed bytecode is immutable.
